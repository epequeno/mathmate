### Core findings

1. **Unguarded AI degrades learning.** Bastani et al. (2025, PNAS) — the flagship
   RCT (~1,000 HS students, Turkey): GPT Base improved practice performance by 48%
   but **decreased unassisted exam performance by 17%**. Students who used AI
   performed worse than those who never had access.

2. **The harm is invisible.** Students don't perceive their own learning
   degradation (Bastani et al.; Yu et al., 2026 — the "speedup illusion").

3. **Design is the differentiator.** Guardrails (hints, not answers; attempt-first
   gates) "essentially eradicated" the crutch effect (Bastani et al.). When AI
   tutoring is meticulously designed around pedagogy, it can outperform
   traditional instruction by 2× (Kestin et al., 2025, Nature).

4. **Substitution is the enemy.** AI that does the work for the student (generating
   solutions) is harmful; AI that supports the student's own effort (explaining,
   hinting) is neutral-to-beneficial (Lehmann et al., 2024).

5. **Gains don't persist.** Multiple studies show AI improves performance while
   available but the improvement doesn't transfer to unassisted performance
   (Stanford SCALE, 2026; Chen et al., 2025).

### What the research validates in MathMate's current design

| Feature | Research support |
|---------|-----------------|
| Hint ladder (4-level escalating hints, attempt-first gate) | Bastani et al. — guardrails "essentially eradicated" the crutch effect |
| Visible reasoning traces | Lehmann et al. — complement > substitution |
| Textbook library | Non-AI reference path — students look things up without the AI doing the work |
| Vault (persistent notes) | Supports complement mode — builds on prior understanding |
| Memory system (SQLite) | Addresses the perception gap — tracks what's mastered vs. struggling |

### The gap identified

The main chat system prompt (`turn/prompt.ts`) says only "Answer as a clear math
tutor" — functionally equivalent to Bastani et al.'s harmful GPT Base arm. The
hint ladder (the protective feature) is opt-in. The default path — the one every
student takes — is unguarded.

### `mathmate/src/lib/turn/prompt.ts` + `mathmate/src/stores/chatStore.ts`
- **Phase 17A — Pedagogical guardrails in system prompt**: Added a `## Pedagogical approach` section to `SYSTEM_INSTRUCTIONS` in both files. The opening line changed from "Answer as a clear math tutor." to "You are a clear math tutor. Your goal is to help the student learn, not just produce correct answers." Six pedagogical rules were added:
  1. Ask what the student has tried before helping with a problem
  2. Prefer guiding questions and next-step hints over complete solutions
  3. Explain why a step works, not just what to do
  4. Answer conceptual questions directly (complement, not substitution)
  5. Show each step explicitly
  6. Flag computational uncertainty and suggest verification

  The guardrails apply to the default tutor style and are inherited by the Olympiad Coach override (which prepends its own stronger scaffolding). No conflict — the Olympiad instructions take precedence where they overlap.

  **Why both files**: `chatStore.ts` has an inline copy of `SYSTEM_INSTRUCTIONS` (the live one that runs), while `prompt.ts` has the extracted/testable copy (Phase 14B extraction). Both were updated to keep them in sync. Technical debt: the chatStore should eventually call `buildSystemPrompt()` from `prompt.ts` instead of maintaining its own copy.
